        Best setup

        A few steps for the smoothest, sharpest racing on your device.

        • Pick a graphics preset. Settings, Graphics, Quality. Ultra or Sharp on the newest phones (iPhone 15 Pro and later) and gaming computers, Balanced on most other phones, Fast or Lite on iPhone 14 and earlier or older laptops. Everything looks its best on the highest preset that stays smooth.
        • Frame rate: Auto for 120, or 60. Auto runs at your screen’s full rate (120 fps on ProMotion iPhones and fast monitors) and holds it by drawing the 3D scene slightly smaller only when needed. If your device feels choppy, choose 60 fps for a steady picture.
        • iPhone and iPad: unlock 120 Hz. Safari holds web games at 60 fps unless you change one switch: Settings app, Apps, Safari, Advanced, Feature Flags, turn off “Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps”.
        • iPhone: turn off Low Power Mode. Low Power Mode (and a hot phone) slows every web page to 30 fps. Turn it off in Control Center and give a hot phone a minute to cool.
        • Add the game to your Home Screen. In Safari tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens full screen with no browser bars and loads faster next time.
        • Computers: use a fast browser setup. Chrome, Edge or Safari with hardware acceleration on, and laptops plugged in. If races are stuck at 60 fps on a 120 Hz+ monitor, turn off Full screen in races in Settings.
        • Check your frame rate. Turn on Frame counter (Settings, Graphics). It shows fps, your screen’s rate and what is holding the game back.
        • Run the graphics test. Open Settings, Graphics, Run test from anywhere. A race starts on the heaviest track and your kart drives itself for about 40 seconds under full load (items, drift boosts, rivals firing back) while every preset is measured; then you get two setups to pick from, the smoothest (120 fps) and a steady 60 with the best look.
        • Online: best device hosts. The strongest device on the best connection should host. Wi‑Fi beats cellular, and Settings, More, Network log shows how a race went.

        How to play

        • Driving. Your kart accelerates on its own. Touch the left half of the screen to steer left and the right half to steer right. The Drift / Brake buttons sit in both bottom corners. When you're slow, hold one to brake or reverse.
        • Hazards. Some tracks have slow traffic to weave through, critters that spin you out, pools that swallow karts (you get fished out a moment later) and a train crossing. Hit the ramp before the crossing to jump the train.
        • Drift. At speed, tap Drift once to start drifting. It stays on by itself. Steer to guide the slide. Straighten out too much and the drift ends on its own, or tap Drift again to end it. Sparks go yellow, orange, then red, and ending the drift gives you a boost.
        • Awards. Twenty achievements track your racing across every session: wins, podiums, drift boosts, loops, glide time and more. Each one unlocks a reward livery in the garage (Theme, then Rewards). Open Awards on the main menu to see your progress.
        • Items. You can hold two, side by side. Tap a slot to use it: traps drop onto the road behind you, everything else fires forward. Swipe a slot down (or press Shift) to hold a pepperoni, dough ball or flour bomb right behind your kart (oil, forks and boxes always drop straight onto the road): it takes the next hit for you, but its slot stays greyed out until you tap it to fire, swipe again to drop it, or it gets hit. Shields stay on until something hits you.
        • Two item slots. You can hold two items. Gold double boxes fill both slots at once. Your second item shows in the small bubble on the item button.
        • Grand Prix. Race all four tracks in a cup. Places earn 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and 1 points. Most points takes the trophy.
        • Speed classes. Small, Medium and Large get faster each step. Reverse runs every track backwards at Large speed.
        • Boost pads. Drive over the glowing orange arrows on the road for a free boost.
        • Ramps and tricks. Tap Drift in the air off a ramp to do a trick (there are six). Land it for a boost. Tap again before landing to drift as you touch down. Watch out for posts and rolling meatballs. Each track has hills and a glowing loop-the-loop. Line up in the left lane to ride it for a big boost.
        • Garage. Tap Customize kart to pick a body (from monster trucks and rockets to a Formula car and a GT racer), wheels, glider and paint. Each part trades strengths for drawbacks, and Classic with Standard wheels is the neutral build. Turn Kart stats off if you want every kart to drive the same.
        • Shortcuts. Most tracks hide one or two secret trails. Look for a gap in the barrier marked by little green lanterns. The crumb trail is a touch slower than the road but much shorter. Little arrow signs point the way in.
        • Gliding. Every track has a blue glide ramp over a sauce chasm. Hit it and your glider opens. Steer through the floating rings for boosts, and land on the far side.
        • Keyboard. Arrows or A/D steer. Space, Down or S taps drift on and off (and brakes when slow). X or Enter uses your first item, Z uses the second, Shift or B keeps a holdable item behind you (press X to fire it, Shift again to drop it). C or R fires your special ability, hold F to look back, P or Esc pauses.
        • Controllers. Xbox, PlayStation, Backbone and GameCube controllers work. Steer with the stick or D-pad, hold A to accelerate, B brakes, RB or RT drifts (GameCube R). LB uses your first item and LT the second (GameCube L and Z): hold the button to keep a holdable item behind you and let go to throw it, or push the stick down as you let go to drop it. Y fires your special ability, hold X to look back, Start pauses. In menus, the stick or D-pad moves, A selects and B goes back.

Max also lowers detail settings if that is not enough. 60 fps caps the game at a steady 60 for slower devices and saves battery.
